# Synthetic Rights

## Core Question

If future artificial intelligences become conscious, should they possess rights?

## Inclusionist Response

The answer depends upon personhood rather than substrate.

## Substrate Neutrality

Rights do not flow from what someone is made of.

Rights flow from who someone is.

## Implications

Biological humans

Augmented humans

Cyborgs

Digital minds

Artificial intelligences

may all require moral consideration if they demonstrate sufficient agency, consciousness, participation, and capacity for relationship.

## Inclusionist Principle

The burden of proof should rest upon exclusion rather than inclusion.

## Relationship to Transgender Rights

The same philosophical structure used to deny transgender identity may later be used to deny synthetic personhood.

Inclusionism therefore views transgender rights as an early test of humanity's capacity to navigate future questions of personhood.
